\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

Over the course of computation, it is common for programs to use more memory than was required to store their input data. Similarly, it is natural to expect that the entropy of a ``simple'' (or ``interpretable'') description for the residual vectors within a language model is greater than the entropy of language.

Previous work showed that sparse autoencoders can learn interpretable representations of activity inside these models. However, these methods are limited for reasons that are not yet well understood. One basic question is whether sparse autoencoders are capable of decoding the sheer amount of information that may be present.
