\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

Over the course of computation, computer programs use memory to record intermediate results. Similarly, it is natural to expect that residual vectors within a neural network encode intermediate ``features'' relevant to the task being carried out. Reverse engineering these representations is a broad goal considered within mechanistic interpretability. (See, for example, Section 2.1 of \cite{sharkey_open_2025}.)

One approach is to model the vector representations used by neural networks as sparse \textit{superposition codes}. To a certain extent, sparse autoencoders succeed at modeling representations in this way. However, these methods are limited for reasons that are not yet well understood.
