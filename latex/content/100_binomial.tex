\section{Estimates for the Binomial Coefficient \label{appendix:binomial}}
To estimate \(\ln \binom{N}{k}\), it is helpful to first remember the elementary inequalities
\[
	\left(\frac{N}{k}\right)^k \le \binom{N}{k} \le \left( \frac{e N}{k} \right)^k.
\]
Taking logarithms gives
\[
	k \ln (N/k) \le \ln \binom{N}{k} \le k \ln (eN/k),
\]
and so $\ln \binom{N}{k} = k \ln (N/k) + O(k).$ In the sublinear regime $\ln k \sim \eta\ln N,$ this proves altogether that
$$
\ln \binom N k \sim (1 - \eta) k \ln N.
$$
This is the approximation we need for our asymptotic results. However, it is also useful to know the finer approximation
$$
\ln \binom N k = k \ln (e N/k) + O(k^2 / N).
$$
If we put $\eta = \ln k / \ln N$ this shows that
$$
\ln \binom N k = (1 - \eta) k \ln N + k + o(1).
$$

One simple heuristic derivation for this estimate is the following. Consider a random subset \(Y \subseteq [N]\) where each element is included independently with probability \(s = k/N\). Then where
$$
h(s) = - s \ln s - (1 - s) \ln (1 - s) \approx - s \ln s + s + O(s^2)
$$
is the binary entropy function, the entropy of \(Y\) is
\begin{align*}
	H(Y) & = h(s) N = s N \ln s^{-1} + s N + O(s^2 N) \\
	     & = k \ln(e N/k) + O(s^2 N) = k \ln (e N / k) + O(k^2/N).
\end{align*}
