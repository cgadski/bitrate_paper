\section{Matched Filters \label{appendix:matched}}

Consider the problem of inferring a scalar $S$ from the sum
$$
Y = S f + Z
$$
where $f \in \R^d$ is fixed and $Z \in \R^d$ is a Gaussian variable independent from $S.$ Suppose for simplicity that $Z$ has non-singular covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \R^{d \times d},$ so that $\ln p(z) = C_\Sigma - \lVert z \rVert_\Sigma^2 / 2$ where $C_\Sigma$ depends only on $\Sigma$ and $\lVert z \rVert_\Sigma^2 = z^T \Sigma^{-1} z.$ In this appendix, we briefly recall how the posterior on $S$ can be written in terms of a certain linear function $\lambda(Y)$ of $Y,$ which we call a matched filter.

Using that $S$ is independent from $Z,$ observe first that
\begin{align}
	\ln p(Y = y \mid S = s)
	 & = \ln p(Z = y - s f \mid S = s) \nonumber \\
	 & = \ln p(Z = y - s f) \nonumber \\
	& = C_\Sigma - \frac 1 2 \lVert y - s f \rVert^2_\Sigma.
\end{align}
Developing the norm $\lVert y - s f \rVert^2_\Sigma$ as a function of $s$ and completing a square gives
\begin{align*}
\lVert y - s f \rVert^2_\Sigma & = \lVert y \rVert^2_\Sigma - 2 \langle y,  f \rangle_\Sigma s + \lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma s^2 \\
& = \lVert y \rVert^2_\Sigma - \frac{\langle y, f \rangle^2}{\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma} + \lVert f \rVert_\Sigma^2 \left (s - \frac{\langle y, f \rangle}{\lVert f \rVert_\Sigma^2} \right)^2,
\end{align*}
where $\langle v, w \rangle_\Sigma =  v^T \Sigma^{-1} w$ is the inner product associated with $\lVert - \rVert_\Sigma.$ Overall,
$$
\ln p(Y = y \mid S = s) = C(y, \Sigma) - \frac {\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma} 2 \left( s - \frac{\langle y, f \rangle}{\lVert f \rVert_\Sigma^2} \right)^2
$$
for a certain $C(y, \Sigma)$ not depending on $s.$ The maximum likelihood estimate for $S$ conditional on $Y$ is therefore
\begin{align*}
	\hat S = \frac{\langle f, Y \rangle_\Sigma}{\lVert f \rVert_\Sigma^2}.
\end{align*}
We call this linear function of $Y$ the matched filter for $S,$ and denote it by $\lambda.$

We can also write the posterior on $S$ in terms of the matched filter, since
\begin{align}
	\ln p(S = s \mid Y = y)
	 & = \ln p(Y = y \mid S = s) - \ln p(Y = y) + \ln p(S = s) \nonumber \\
	 & = C(y, \Sigma) - \ln p(Y = y) + \ln p(s) - \frac {\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma} 2 \left( s - \lambda(y) \right)^2.
\end{align}
Finally, note that the norm $\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma$ is exactly the inverse variance of the noise $Z$ under the matched filter. Indeed, since we have
$$
\Var \, \langle w, Z \rangle = w^T \Sigma w
$$
for any vector $w \in \R^d,$
\begin{align*}
\Var\, \lambda(Z) & = \Var \, \frac{\langle f, Z \rangle_\Sigma}{\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma}  = \frac{1}{\lVert f \rVert_\Sigma^4} \Var \, \langle \Sigma^{-1} f, Z \rangle \\
& = \frac{f^T \Sigma^{-1} \Sigma \Sigma^{-1} f}{\lVert f \rVert_\Sigma^4} = \frac{1}{\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma}.
\end{align*}

In the parlance of signal processing, we could call $\lVert f \rVert^2_\Sigma$ the ``signal-to-noise ratio'' of the filter $\lambda,$ since it measures ratio between the magnitude $\lambda(f) = 1$ of its sensitivity to the signal $S$ and the magnitude $\Var\, \lambda(Z)$ of its sensitivity to the noise.
