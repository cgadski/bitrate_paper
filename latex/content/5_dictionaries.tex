\section{Are Random Dictionaries Optimal? \label{sec:dictionaries}}

This work has considered the performance of different decoding methods in a scenario where the dictionary $F$ was randomly generated. Of course, the dictionaries employed by neural networks may have special structure. It is natural to ask whether our findings would be significantly different if the dictionary were specially designed in some way.

One natural measurement for the coherence of a dictionary is the mean squared interference of codewords
$$
\xi(F) = \binom N 2 ^{-1} \sum_{i < j} \langle f_i, f_j \rangle^2.
$$
Indeed, if we assume an isotropic distribution of codewords, from the point of view of \Cref{sec:codes} the mean squared interference controls the average signal-to-noise ratio of the ``filters'' $\lambda_i(Y) = \langle f_i, Y \rangle.$

An important result in coding theory known as the Welch bound \citep{welch_lower_1974} states that, for any dictionary $F \in \R^{d \times N}$ of unit norm codewords, we have
$$
\sum_{i, j} \langle f_i, f_j \rangle^2 \ge \frac {N^2} d.
$$
This can be derived by viewing the sum of squared inner products above as the sum of squared eigenvalues $\lambda_i^2$ of the Gram matrix $F^T F$ and applying the inequality $\sum_i \lambda_i^2 \ge N \sum_i \lambda_i.$

In terms of mean squared interference $\xi(F),$ the Welch bound means that
$$
\xi(F) \ge \frac 1 {N^2 - N} \left( \frac {N^2} d - N \right) = \frac 1 d \left ( 1 - \frac d N \right) \left( 1 + \frac 1 {N - 1} \right).
$$
Therefore, in a limit where $N \to \infty$ and $d/N \to 0,$ we have $\xi(F) \ge 1/d + o(1).$ However, recall that for a pair $(F_i, F_j)$ of independent random unit-norm codewords we have
$$
\E \langle F_i, F_j \rangle^2 = \frac 1 d.
$$
(This is true for both spherical and Rademacher dictionaries.) Therefore, $\E \xi(F) = 1/d$ for a dictionary of random codewords. In the sublinear regime $k \le N^\eta$ for $\eta < 1,$ the previous section showed that one-step decoders are viable in regimes where $d/N \to 0,$ so in this situation we conclude that mean squared interference $\xi(F)$ cannot be significantly improved. It stands as a conjecture that the performance of MAP thresholding and top-$k$ in any sublinear regime is not significantly improved by any family of structured dictionaries.
