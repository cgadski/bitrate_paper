\section{Derivation of the Maximum a Posteriori Threshold} \label{appendix:threshold}

For a given index $i$, recall that $Z_i = \sum_{j \neq i} X_j f_j$ is the nuisance term in the problem of estimating $X_i$ from
$$
Y = F X = X_i f_i + Z_i.
$$
In \Cref{sec:threshold-top-k}, we stated that this estimation can be performed by replacing $Z_i$ with a Gaussian variable $\tilde Z.$ In this section, we detail the form of the maximum a posteriori estimate for $X_i$ under this simplified model.

First, let us determine the appropriate variance for $\tilde Z$ by calculating the variance of $Z_i.$ Suppose that the codewords $F_i$ are isotropically distributed random variables, meaning $\Var\, F_i = \alpha I$ for some scalar $\alpha > 0.$ This is true for Rademacher and spherical dictionaries, for instance. If in addition the codewords are unit vectors, then necessarily we have $\alpha = 1/d$ since
$$
1 = \lVert F_i \rVert^2 = \E\, \lVert F_i \rVert^2 = \tr \Sigma = d \alpha.
$$
Conditional on $X_i = 0,$ $Z_i$ is a sum of $k$ independent codewords, and so its coordinates have variance $k/d.$ Meanwhile, conditional on $X_i = 1,$ the coordinates of $Z_i$ have variance $(k - 1)/d.$ Since $\Pp(X_i = 1) = k/N,$ overall $\Var\, Z_i = \beta I$ with
$$
\beta = \frac{N - k}{N} \cdot \frac k d + \frac k N \cdot \frac{k - 1}{d} = \frac{k}{Nd}\big[(N - k) + (k - 1)\big] = \frac{N - 1}{N} \frac k d.
$$
Thus, we consider that $\tilde Z$ is an isotropic centered Gaussian where each coordinate has variance $(N - 1)k/Nd.$

Because $\tilde Z$ is isotropic and $\lVert f_i \rVert = 1,$ it follows from \Cref{appendix:matched} that the maximum likelihood estimator is $\lambda_i(Y) = \langle f_i, Y \rangle,$ with signal-to-noise ratio
$$
\rho_i = \left( \Var\, \langle f_i, \tilde Z \rangle \right)^{-1} = \left( \frac{(N - 1)k}{Nd} \lVert f_i \rVert^2 \right)^{-1} = \frac N {N - 1} \frac{d}{k}.
$$

By \Cref{appendix:matched}, the posterior on $X_i$ under the simplified Gaussian model is
$$
\ln \Pp(X_i = x \mid Y = y) = -\frac{\rho_i}{2} \left( x - \lambda_i(y) \right)^2 + \ln \Pp(X_i = x) + C,
$$
where $C$ does not depend on $x.$ The maximum a posteriori estimate sets $\hat X_i(y) = 1$ exactly when $\ln \Pp(X_i = 1 \mid Y = y) \ge \ln \Pp(X_i = 0 \mid Y = y).$ Cancelling $C$ and the common term $\rho_i \lambda_i(y)^2 / 2,$ this condition becomes $\lambda_i(y) \ge \tau,$ where
$$
\tau = \frac 1 2 - \frac{1}{\rho_i} \ln \frac{\Pp(X_i = 1)}{\Pp(X_i = 0)}.
$$
Substituting the prior that $\Pp(X = 1) = k/N$ gives
\begin{equation}
\tau = \frac 1 2 - \rho_i^{-1} \ln \frac k {N - k} = \frac 1 2 + \frac{N - 1} N \frac{k}{d} \ln \frac{N - k}{k}. \label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}

Since we expect $N \gg k,$ it is reasonable to drop terms of order $O(k/N).$ Using that
$$
\ln \frac{N - k}{k} = \ln \frac N k + \ln\left( 1 - \frac k N \right) = \ln N - \ln k + O(k/N),
$$
we can expand (\ref{eq:tau}) as
\begin{align*}
\tau
& = \frac 1 2 + \frac{N - 1} N \frac{k}{d} \ln \frac{N - k}{k} \\
& = \frac 1 2 + \left(1 + O\!\left(\tfrac 1 N \right)\right) \frac k d \left( \ln N - \ln k + O\!\left(\tfrac k N\right) \right) \\
& = \frac 1 2 + \frac k d \left( \ln N - \ln k \right) + O\!\left( \frac{k \ln N}{Nd} \right) + O\!\left( \frac{k^2}{Nd} \right).
\end{align*}
Since we necessarily have $d = \Omega(k \ln N),$ both terms are $O(k/N),$ which is negligible in practice. Overall, we are left with the MAP thresholding value of
$$
\tau_\text{MAP} = \frac k d \left( \ln N - \ln k \right) = \frac 1 2 + \left(1 - \frac{\ln k}{\ln N} \right) \frac{k \ln N}{d}.
$$
