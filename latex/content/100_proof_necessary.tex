\section{Proof of \Cref{prop:necessary-cond} \label{appendix:necessary}}

In \Cref{sec:information}, we recalled that information theory can establish bounds on the amount of information that can be transmitted over a noisy channel. These bounds are called channel capacities. For instance, when a random vector $Y \in \R^d$ with coordinates $Y_i$ satisfying $\Var(Y_i) < 1$ is corrupted by addition of a Gaussian vector $Z$ with independent coordinates each of variance $P,$ the resulting ``transmission'' can convey at most $d \ln(1 + P^{-1})/2$ nats of information to a receiver. More precisely, in terms of mutual information, this means that
$$
I(Y; Y + Z) \le \frac d 2 \ln (1 + P^{-1})
$$
so long as $Y$ and $Z$ are independent. For large $P,$ note that $\ln(1 + P^{-1}) = P^{-1} + O(P^{-2}),$ so in this case the channel capacity is approximately $d/(2P).$ Finally, it can be shown that $I(Y; Y + Z) = \frac d 2 \ln(1 + P^{-1})$ exactly when the coordinates of $Y$ are independent unit Gaussians. For a reference on these facts, we again suggest \cite{cover_elements_2006}.

Mutual information bounds of this form are the basic tool underlying our proof of \Cref{prop:necessary-cond}. Specifically, we will use the following bound on the capacity attainable by a vector in superposition with Rademacher codewords. (Recall that, by our earlier definition of a Rademacher dictionary, Rademacher codewords are distributed uniformly over $\{-1/\sqrt{d}, 1/\sqrt{d} \}^d.$)

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:mutual}
Let $Z \in \R^d$ be a sum of $k$ independent Rademacher codewords. Then for any random vector $Y \in \R^{d}$ supported on $\{-1/\sqrt{d}, 1/\sqrt{d}\}^d$ and independent from $Z,$
$$
I(Y; Y + Z) \le d \left( \frac 1 {2 k} + O(k^{-2}) \right).
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because $Y$ and $Z$ are independent, $H(Y + Z \mid Y) = H(Z).$ This lets us rewrite the mutual information as a difference of entropies:
$$
I(Y; Y + Z) = H(Y + Z) - H(Y + Z \mid Y) = H(Y + Z) - H(Z).
$$
Since the coordinates of $Z$ are independent, $H(Z) = \sum_i H(Z_i).$ Furthermore, by subadditivity of entropy,
$$
H(Y + Z) \le \sum_i H(Y_i + Z_i),
$$
with equality when the coordinates of $Y_i$ are independent. We will prove that, for each $i,$
$$
H(Y_i + Z_i) - H(Z_i) \le \frac{1}{2 k} + O(k^{-2}).
$$
Recall that $Z_i$ is a sum of $k$ independent uniform signs $\pm 1/\sqrt{d},$ while $Y_i$ is supported on $\{-1/\sqrt{d}, 1/\sqrt{d}\}$ and independent of $Z_i.$

First, we claim that $H(Y_i + Z_i)$ is maximized when $Y_i$ is uniform on $\{-1/\sqrt{d}, 1/\sqrt{d}\}.$ To see why, parameterize the distribution of $Y_i$ by $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ such that $\Pp(Y_i = -1/\sqrt{d}) = \lambda,$ and define the function $f \colon [0, 1] \to \R$ by
$$
f(\lambda) = H(Y_i + Z_i).
$$
Because entropy is a concave function of probabilities and $\Pp(Y_i + Z_i = x)$ is an affine function of $\lambda$ for each $x,$ $f$ is concave. Furthermore, because $Z_i$ is distributed like $-Z_i,$
$$
f(\lambda) = H(Y_i + Z_i) = H(-Y_i - Z_i) = H(- Y_i + Z_i) = f(1 - \lambda).
$$
From these two facts, $f$ must attain its maximum value at $\lambda = 1/2.$

To bound $H(Y_i + Z_i) - H(Z_i),$ it therefore suffices to consider the case that $Y_i$ is uniform. Using independence again, it is convenient to regard this difference as a mutual information:
$$
H(Y_i + Z_i) - H(Z_i) = H(Y_i + Z_i) - H(Y_i + Z_i \mid Y_i) = I(Y_i; Y_i + Z_i).
$$
To compute $I(Y_i; Y_i + Z_i)$, observe that $Y_i + Z_i$ is a sum of $k + 1$ independent uniform signs $\pm 1/\sqrt{d}.$ Conditional on $Y_i + Z_i = w,$ exactly $(\sqrt{d} w + k + 1)/2$ of these $k + 1$ signs are positive, and by symmetry, each summand is equally likely to be among the positive ones. The posterior on $Y_i$ is therefore
$$
\Pp\!\left(Y_i = 1/\sqrt{d} \mid Y_i + Z_i\right) = \frac{\sqrt{d} (Y_i + Z_i) + k + 1}{2(k + 1)} = \frac 1 2 + \frac{\sqrt{d} (Y_i + Z_i)}{2(k + 1)}.
$$
Putting $S = \sqrt{d} (Y_i + Z_i)/(2(k+1))$ and writing $h$ for the binary entropy function, we find that
$$
I(Y_i; Y_i + Z_i) = H(Y_i) - H(Y_i \mid Y_i + Z_i) = \ln 2 - \E \left[ h\!\left(\frac{1}{2} + S\right)\right].
$$
Taking a series expansion of $h$ at $1/2$ gives
$$
I(Y_i; Y_i + Z_i) = \E \left[ 2 S^2 + O(S^4)\right].
$$

Note that $S$ is distributed like $R_{k + 1} / (2(k + 1)),$ where $R_{k + 1}$ is a sum of $(k + 1)$ Rademacher variables. Recall that the moments of this sum satisfy
$$
\E[R_{k + 1}^2] = k + 1, \quad \E[R_{k + 1}^4] = O(k^2).
$$
We conclude that
$$
I(Y_i; Y_i + Z_i) = 2 \E[S^2] + O(\E[S^4]) = \frac{1}{2(k+1)} + O(k^{-2}) = \frac{1}{2k} + O(k^{-2}).
$$
\end{proof}

It will also be useful to state Fano's lemma in the following way.

\begin{lemma} \label{lemma:fano}
Let $X$ be uniformly distributed over $[N]$. Then for any random variable $Y$ and any function $f,$
$$
\Pp(f(Y) \neq X) \ge 1 - \frac{\ln 2 + I(X ; Y)}{\ln N}.
$$
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A usual statement of Fano's lemma is that, if there exists some function $f$ satisfying $f(Y) = X$ with probability $p,$ then
$$
H(X \mid Y) \le h(p) + (1 - p) \ln N,
$$
where $h(p)$ is the binary entropy function. Since $h(p) \le \ln 2$ and $H(X) = \ln N,$ this implies that
$$
\Pp(f(Y) = X) = p \le 1 - \frac{H(X \mid Y) - \ln 2}{\ln N} = \frac{\ln 2 + \ln N - H(X \mid Y)}{\ln N} = \frac{\ln 2 + I(X; Y)}{\ln N},
$$
which gives our statement above.
\end{proof}

Now we proceed to the proof of \Cref{prop:necessary-cond}, restated for convenience.

\begin{proposition*}
    Let $C > 0$ be arbitrary. Over any regime where
    $$
    d \le C k \ln N, \quad \omega(1) \le k < N/2,
    $$
    for Rademacher dictionaries it holds that
    $$
    \liminf_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N; \text{top-$k$}) \ge 1 - \frac C 2.
    $$
\end{proposition*}

This is a consequence of the following explicit bound on the failure probability $b(d, k, N; \text{top-$k$})$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma:explicit}
For all integers $d \ge 1, k \ge 1$ and $N \ge 1,$
$$
b(d, k + 1, N + k; \text{top-}k) \ge 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{\ln N} - \frac{d}{2 k \ln N}(1 + O(k^{-1})).
$$
\end{lemma}

Indeed, given any $k \ge 2$ and $N \ge k,$ invoking \Cref{lemma:explicit} with the change of variables
$$
k' = k - 1, \quad N' = N - k + 1
$$
gives a lower bound on $b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k)$:
\begin{align*}
b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k) & = b(d, k' + 1, N' + k'; \text{top-}k)  \\
& \ge 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{\ln (N - k + 1)} - \frac{d (1 + O(k^{-1}))}{2 (k - 1) \ln (N - k + 1)}.
\end{align*}
Under the regime $d \le C k \ln N$ and $\omega(1) \le k \le N/2,$
$$
\frac{d (1 + O(k^{-1}))}{2(k - 1) \ln(N - k + 1)} = \frac{d}{2 k \ln N} \frac{1 + O(k^{-1})}{1 + o(1)} \le \frac{C}{2} + o(1)
$$
and of course
$$
\frac{\ln 2}{\ln (N - k + 1)} = o(1).
$$
We conclude overall that
$$
b(d, k, N, \text{top-}k) \ge 1 - \frac{C}{2} + o(1),
$$
which proves our result. We proceed to prove \Cref{lemma:explicit}.

\begin{proof}
Define three independent random variables:
\begin{itemize}
\item Let $F \in \R^{d \times (N + k)}$ be a Rademacher dictionary;
\item Let $S$ be uniformly random in $[N]$;
\item Let $\sigma$ be a random permutation of $[N + k]$.
\end{itemize}
Define the vector
$$
X_i = \begin{cases}
1 : i = S \lor i > N \\
0 : \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
We write $(F^0, F^1)$ for the first $N$ and last $k$ coordinates of $F,$ and similarly write $(X^0, X^1)$ for the first $N$ and last $k$ coordinates of $X.$ Thus, the superposition code for $X$ under $F$ can be written as
$$
Y = F X = F^0 X^0 + F^1 X^1.
$$
The vector $F^0 X^0 = F_S$ is the $S$th codeword of $F,$ and $F^1 X^1$ is a superposition of the last $k$ codewords of $F.$ We will write $\sigma(X)$ and $\sigma(F)$ for the permutations of $X$ and $F$ under $\sigma$ defined in the natural way. Under these conditions, $(\sigma(X), \sigma(F))$ is an independent pair of a uniformly random $(k + 1)$-sparse vector and a Rademacher dictionary.

Now, define a function $g \colon \R^{d \times N} \times \R^d \to [N]$ by
$$
g(F, y) = \underset{i \in [N]}{\arg \max} \, \langle F^0_i, y \rangle,
$$
We claim that $g(F, Y) = S$ with at least the same probability that the top-$k$ algorithm recovers $\sigma(X)$ from the input $(Y, \sigma(F)).$ Indeed, if top-$k$ succeeds, then it must be the case that
$$
\langle F_S, Y \rangle \ge \langle F_i, Y \rangle
$$
for all $i \in [N],$ because otherwise top-$k$ would select an index in the set $\sigma([N] \setminus \{S \}),$ while $\sigma(X)$ is zero at these indices. Under these conditions, it is clear that $g(F, Y) = S$ with average probability no smaller than the probability that top-$k$ succeeds. Moreover, since $(\sigma(X), \sigma(F))$ are distributed as in our definition of the function $b(d, k+1, N+k; \text{top-}k),$ this proves that
$$
b(d, k + 1, N + k; \text{top-}k) \ge \Pp(g(F^0, F X) \neq S).
$$
Now we will lower bound the probability $\Pp(g(F^0, F X) \neq S)$ by combining Fano's inequality (in the form of \Cref{lemma:fano}) with our upper bound on mutual information (\Cref{prop:mutual}).

Applying \Cref{lemma:fano} to the map $g,$ we have
$$
\Pp(g(F^0, FX) \neq S) \ge 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{\ln N} - \frac{I(S; (F^0, FX))}{\ln N}.
$$
Since $F^0$ is independent from $S,$ $I(S; (F^0, FX)) = I(S; FX \mid F^0).$ Furthermore, because the conditional distribution of $FX$ given $S$ and $F^0$ depends only on $F_S,$ $I(S; F X \mid F^0) = I(F_S; F X \mid F^0).$ All together,
$$
I(S; (F^0, FX)) =  I(F_S; FX \mid F^0).
$$
Conditional on $F^0,$ we can apply \Cref{prop:mutual} to the pair $(F_S, FX),$ since $F_S \in \{-1/\sqrt{d}, 1/\sqrt{d} \}^d$ and $FX = F_S + F^1 X^1$ is a sum of $F_S$ with $k$ independent Rademacher vectors. Since this bound holds independently of $F^0,$ we conclude that overall
$$
I(F_S; F X \mid F^0) \le d \left(\frac 1 {2 k} + O(k^{-2})\right).
$$
Altogether,
\begin{align*}
b(d, k + 1, N + k; \text{top-}k) & \ge \Pp(g(F^0, FX) \neq S) \\
& \ge 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{\ln N} - \frac{d}{\ln N} \left( \frac 1 {2 k} + O(k^{-2}) \right) \\
& = 1 - \frac{\ln 2}{\ln N} - \frac{d}{2 k \ln N} (1 + O(k^{-1}))
\end{align*}
as desired.
\end{proof}
