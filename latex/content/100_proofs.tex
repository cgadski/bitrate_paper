\section{Proofs of Main Results \label{appendix:proofs}}

We begin with \Cref{prop:necessary-cond}, restated here for convenience. Recall that $b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k)$ is the probability that the top-$k$ decoder accurately recovers a $k$-sparse subset of $[N]$ from a $d$-dimensional superposition code using a random spherical dictionary.

\begin{proposition*}
    Let $\epsilon > 0$ be arbitrary. Over the regime
    $$
    d \le (1 - \epsilon) 2 k \ln N, \quad 2 \le k < N/2,
    $$
    it holds that $\liminf_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N; \text{top-$k$}) > 0.$
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}
We can suppose w.l.o.g.\ that $X$ is the set $\{1, \dots, k \}.$ As before, we write $F$ for the dictionary, $F_i$ for its constituent codewords, and $Y = F X = F_1 + \dots + F_k$ for the superposition code.

It is easy to show that, with probability bounded below by a constant independent of $(d, k),$ we have $\lVert Y \rVert > \sqrt k$ and $\langle F_1, Y \rangle < 1$ so long as $k > 2.$

Let us condition on this event. The remaining codewords $F_{k + 1}, \dots, F_N$ are independent, uniform draws from the unit sphere. Let $B_i$ be the event that $\langle F_i, Y \rangle \ge 1,$ and let $B$ be the union of the $(N - k)$ events $\{B_{k + 1}, \dots, B_N \}.$ If $B$ holds, then top-$k$ decoding will not correctly recover $X$ from the code $Y.$

For each $i \in \{k+1, \dots, N\}$, we have
$$\Pp(B_i) = \Pp(\langle F_i, Y \rangle \ge 1) = \Pp\left(\left\langle F_i, \frac{Y}{\lVert Y \rVert} \right\rangle \ge \frac{1}{\lVert Y \rVert}\right).$$

For each $i > k$, the pair $(Y_i, Y / \lVert Y \rVert)$ is a pair of independent, uniform draws from the unit sphere, so combining \Cref{prop:ball} with our assumption $\lVert Y \rVert \ge \sqrt k$ gives
$$\Pp(B_i) \ge \exp\left(-\frac{d}{2(\lVert Y \rVert^2 - 1)}\right) > \exp\left(-\frac{d}{2(k - 1)}\right).$$

Since the events $B_i$ are independent, we can lower bound $\Pp(B)$ as
$$
\Pp(B) = \Pp\left( \bigcup_i B_i \right) = 1 - \prod_{i = k + 1}^N (1 - \Pp(B_i)) \ge 1 - \exp \left( \sum_i -\Pp(B_i) \right).
$$
Substituting in our lower bound on $\Pp(B_i)$ gives
$$\Pp(B) \ge 1 - \exp\left(-(N - k) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{d}{2(k-1)}\right)\right).$$
When $d \le (1 - \epsilon) 2k \ln N$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
\Pp(B)
&\ge 1 - \exp\left(-(N - k) \exp\left(-\frac{(1-\epsilon) k \ln N}{k-1}\right)\right)\\
&\ge 1 - \exp\left(-(N - k) N^{1 - \epsilon }\right).
\end{align*}
Finally, by our assumptions $k < N/2$ and $\epsilon > 0,$ we conclude that
$$
b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k) \ge P(B) \ge 1 - \exp\left(- \frac 1 2 N^\epsilon \right) \xrightarrow{N \to \infty} 1.
$$
\end{proof}
