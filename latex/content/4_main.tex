For a given family of dictionaries $F_{N, d} \in \R^{N \times d},$ and consider the problem of recovering a random subset $k$-element subset $X$ from a $d$-dimensional superposition code. Let
$$
b(d, k, N; \tau), \quad b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k)
$$
denote the respective failure probabilities for threshold decoding at level $\tau$ and for top-$k$ decoding.\footnote{The letter $b$ is a mnemonic for the ``bad event.''} Naturally, we have
$$
b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k) \le b(d, k, N; \tau),
$$
since if $Y$ is decoded by threshold decoding at any level, it is also decodable by top-$k.$

Our first result provides an asymptotic constraint on the dimension $d$ required for either of these values to be converge to $0$ for large $N.$

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:necessary-cond}
    Let $\epsilon > 0$ be arbitrary. Over the regime
    $$
    d \le (1 - \epsilon) 2 k \ln N,
    $$
    it holds that $\liminf_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k) > 0.$
\end{proposition}


Now, consider this result from an information-theoretic view. In a regime when $k \ge \epsilon N$ for some $\epsilon > 0$, called a regime of linear sparsity, the entropy of $X$ is
$$
H = \ln \binom{N}{k} \ge k \ln \left( \frac N k \right) = O(N).
$$
However, \Cref{prop:necessary-cond} shows that we need at least $d = \Omega(k \ln N) = \Omega(N \ln N)$ dimensions for either threshold decoding or top-$k$ to read a superposition code, which is asymptotically larger. In this regime, the amount of information effectively transmitted by each dimension of the code diverges to zero.

Is it natural to ask whether, in some asymptotic regime, a superposition code can carry a non-vanishing amount of information per dimension. By \Cref{prop:necessary-cond}, we would need $H / k \ln N$ to be bounded below. Since $\ln \binom N k = k \ln (N/k) + O(k),$ as $N \to \infty$ we have
$$
\frac{H} {k \ln N} = \frac{k \ln N - k \ln k + O(k)}{k \ln N} = 1 - \frac{\ln N} {\ln k} + O\left(\frac 1 {\ln N}\right).
$$
This leads us to focus on the regime $k \le N^\eta$ for $\eta < 1,$ which is known as the \textit{sublinear regime}.

Our next result shows that

\begin{proposition} \label{prop:sufficient-cond}
	For $\eta \in (0, 1),$ let $\ln k \le \eta \ln N.$ Then for any $\epsilon > 0,$ over the regime
	$$
	d \ge (1 + \epsilon) (2 + 4 \sqrt \eta + 2 \eta) k \ln N,
	$$
	it holds that $\lim_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N) = 0.$
\end{proposition}



\begin{lemma}
For all $d, k, N \in \N$ and $\tau \in (0, 1),$
$$
b(d, k, N; \tau) \le
k \exp\left( - \frac{(1 - \tau)^2 d}{2 (k - 1)} \right) + (N - k) \exp\left( - \frac{\tau^2 d} {2 k} \right)
$$
\end{lemma}


This result has a remarkable interpretation in terms of effective ``channel capacity.''
