\section{Main Results \label{sec:main}}

\color{blue} We are now ready to state our main results. \color{black} For a given family of dictionaries $F_{N, d} \in \R^{d \times N},$ consider the problem of recovering a random $k$-element subset $X$ from a $d$-dimensional superposition code. We will write
$$
b(d, k, N; \tau), \quad b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k)
$$
for respective failure probabilities of threshold decoding at level $\tau$ and top-$k$ decoding.\footnote{The letter $b$ is a mnemonic for ``bad event.''} \color{blue} (In our theoretical results, we exclusively consider Rademacher dictionaries.) \color{black} Naturally we have $b(d, k, N; \text{top-}k) \le b(d, k, N; \tau),$ since if a given code $Y$ is accurately decoded by threshold decoding at any level, it is also decoded by top-$k.$

Our first result provides an asymptotic constraint on the dimension $d$ required for either of these values to converge to $0$ for large $N.$

\color{blue} \begin{proposition} \label{prop:necessary-cond}
    Let $C > 0$ be arbitrary. Over any regime where
    $$
    d \le C k \ln N, \quad \omega(1) \le k < N/2,
    $$
    for Rademacher dictionaries it holds that
    $$
    \liminf_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N; \text{top-$k$}) \ge 1 - \frac C 2.
    $$
\end{proposition}

\color{blue} Here, $\omega(1)$ denotes any function that diverges to infinity for large $N.$ Our proof (available in \Cref{appendix:necessary}) is based on an information-theoretic argument, similar to a typical proof of \Cref{prop:information}. \color{black}

Now, in a regime when $k \ge \epsilon N$ for some $\epsilon > 0$, the entropy of a $k$-element subset of $[N]$ is
$$
H = \ln \binom{N}{k} \le k \ln \left( \frac {eN} k \right) = O(N).
$$
However, \Cref{prop:necessary-cond} shows that we need at least $d = \Omega(k \ln N) = \Omega(N \ln N)$ dimensions for top-$k$ to read a superposition code for this set. In this regime, often called a regime of \textit{linear sparsity}, we can informally say that the amount of information that top-$k$ can read from each dimension of the code converges to $0.$

Is it natural to ask whether top-$k$ can be ``information-efficient'' in some sparser regime. By our discussion, we would need $H / (k \ln N)$ to be bounded strictly above $0.$ Since
$$
\frac{H} {k \ln N} = \frac{k \ln N - k \ln k + O(k)}{k \ln N} = 1 - \frac{\ln k} {\ln N} + O\left(\frac 1 {\ln N}\right),
$$
we conclude that top-$k$ can only be information-efficient in a regime where $\ln k / \ln N$ is bounded strictly below 1. This motives the introduction of a parameter $\eta = \ln k / \ln N,$ which we call the sparsity exponent. In compressive sensing, when $k \le N^\eta$ for some fixed $\eta < 1$ we say we are in a regime of \textit{sublinear sparsity}. Our next result gives a sufficient condition for threshold decoding to succeed under sublinear sparsity.

\color{blue}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sufficient-cond}
    For $\eta \in (0, 1],$ let $k \le N^\eta.$ Then for any $\epsilon > 0,$ over the regime
	$$
	d \ge (1 + \epsilon) 2(1 + \sqrt \eta)^2 k \ln N, \quad \tau \in \left [\frac 1 2 + (1 - \eta) \frac{k \ln N} d, (1 + \sqrt \eta)^{-1} \right],
	$$
	for Rademacher dictionaries it holds that
	$$
	\lim_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N; \tau) = 0.
	$$
\end{proposition}

Note that the lower bound on $\tau$ in this statement is identical to the MAP decoding threshold $\tau_\text{MAP}$ derived in \Cref{sec:threshold-top-k}. See \Cref{appendix:sufficient} for a proof of \Cref{prop:necessary-cond}. \color{black}

In the extreme case where $\eta = 1,$ the condition $k \le N^\eta$ is vacuous and overall \Cref{prop:sufficient-cond} lets us conclude that $d \ge (1 + \epsilon) 8 k \ln N$ dimensions are enough for threshold decoding to succeed with threshold $\tau = 1/2$ for any $k$ as $N \to \infty.$ For smaller values of $\eta,$ \Cref{prop:sufficient-cond} shows that we can improve the constant factor by choosing a threshold $\tau$ strictly larger than $1/2.$ As $\eta$ approaches $0,$ our result indicates it is appropriate to choose a threshold $\tau$ approaching $1.$

\Cref{fig:k-sweep} shows how the critical value $d_\text{crit} = 2 (1 + \sqrt \eta)^2 k \ln N$ appearing in the statement of \Cref{prop:sufficient-cond} compares with empirical performance of MAP thresholding and top-$k$ for finite values of $N$ and $k.$ Note that the statement of our result is asymptotic, and so does not give guarantees on the performance of either decoding method for finite $(k, N).$ (More explicit statements can be derived from \Cref{lemma:b-bound} of \Cref{appendix:sufficient}, which provides an explicit upper bound on $b(d, k, N; \tau).$) However, $d_\text{crit}$ serves empirically as a good rule of thumb; for $k < 64$ and for values of $N$ ranging over several orders of magnitude, $d_\text{crit}$ can be used to reliably predict the performance of both MAP thresholding and top-$k.$ In particular, although we find top-$k$ is noticeably more reliable, the sample complexities of both methods are similar and grow as a function of $k$ and $N$ in a similar way.

Now we return to the information-theoretic point of view. Using the fact that
$$
\frac H {k \ln N} = \frac{\ln \binom N k}{k \ln N} = 1 - \eta + O\left ( \frac 1 {\ln N} \right),
$$
we can summarize the conclusions of \Cref{prop:necessary-cond} and \Cref{prop:sufficient-cond} in the following way.

\color{blue} \begin{corollary} \label{prop:information-statement}
For each $\eta \in (0, 1)$ and $m \in \{ \text{top-}k, \text{MAP} \},$ let $C(\eta; m)$ be the infimum of all constants $C$ such that, over all regimes satisfying $k \sim N^\eta$ and $d / H > C,$ we have
$$
\lim_{N \to \infty} b(d, k, N; m) = 0.
$$
Then, for Rademacher dictionaries,
$$
\frac{2}{1 - \eta} \le C(\eta; \text{top-}k) \le C(\eta; \text{MAP}) \le
\frac{2(1 + \sqrt \eta)}{1 - \sqrt \eta} = \frac{2(1 + \sqrt \eta)^2}{1 - \eta}.
$$
\end{corollary}

Informally, we may interpret $C(\eta; m)$ as the number of dimensions required to transmit each nat of information as $N \to \infty$ and $k \sim N^\eta.$
In the limit $\eta \to 0$ note that these two expressions match; we find that $2$ dimensions per nat (around $1.4$ dimensions per bit) is necessary for top-$k$ decoding and sufficient for MAP decoding. Thus, these two methods have similar sample complexities when the vector $X$ is extremely sparse. Furthermore, since the lower bound of $2 / (1 - \eta)$ is increasing in $\eta,$ we know that both $C(\eta; \text{top-}k)$ and $C(\eta; \text{MAP})$ are larger than $2$ when $\eta > 0.$

Now, let us compare our theoretical results on the constants $C(\eta; m)$ with empirical performance of top-$k$, MAP decoding, and GMP. To estimate the entropy $H = \ln \binom k N $ of the variable $X$ in the setting that $k \le N^\eta$ for $\eta < 1/2,$ it will be helpful to know that \dots
$$
\tilde H = k \ln (e N/k) = \ln \binom N k + O(N^{2 \eta - 1}).
$$
(See \Cref{appendix:binomial} for a derivation.) For example, when $N=2^{16}$ and $k \le 5000,$ the relative error of this approximation is smaller than $1.4\%.$ We will write $\tilde H_2 = \tilde H / \ln 2$ for the expression of $\tilde H$ in bits.

\caption{\textbf{How many dimensions do we need to store one bit of information?} We graph empirical performance of MAP thresholding, top-$k$, and GMP at exactly decoding a superposition code as a function of approximate inverse bitrate $d / \tilde H_2 = d / (k \log_2(e N/k))$ and sparsity exponent $\eta = \ln k / \ln N.$ \color{blue} The dashed line gives the relation $d / \tilde H = 2 (1 + \sqrt \eta) / (1 - \sqrt \eta)$ representing the upper bound given by \Cref{prop:information-statement}. \color{black} \Cref{fig:eta-sweep} shows the empirical performance of different decoding methods in terms of the (approximate) inverse bitrate $d/\tilde H_2$ and the exponent $\eta = \ln k / \ln N,$ as $N$ ranges over several orders of magnitude and $\eta \in [0, 0.4].$ Results on the bottom row are truncated where they would require a dictionary with dimensions larger than $4096 \times 2^{20}.$

The two left-most columns of \Cref{fig:eta-sweep} show empirical performance of MAP decoding and top-$k$, previously reported in \Cref{fig:k-sweep}, in the $(\eta, d/\tilde H_2)$ coordinate system. The upper and lower bounds on the asymptotic constants $C(\eta; m)$ indicated in \Cref{prop:information-statement} are represented by dashed and dotted lines respectively. We find that performance of both methods is comparable to the asymptotically sufficient bitrate $2 (1 + \sqrt \eta) / (1 - \sqrt \eta).$ Note that, with the values $N = 2^{20}$ and $k = 64$ we referenced from \citet{gao_scaling_2024}, we have $\eta = \ln k / \ln N = 0.3.$

The next three columns of \Cref{fig:eta-sweep} show the performance of generalized matching pursuit (GMP). We find that, even with $T = 3$ steps, GMP succeeds reliably with $d/\tilde H_2 > 2$ over the range $\eta \in (0, 0.35).$ In the case $N = 2^{20}$ and $k = 64,$ three-step GMP decreases the required dimensions by a factor of more than $2.$ Increasing the step parameter further shows noticeable but diminishing returns. Overall, we find that a relatively small increase in computation has outsized effects on sample complexity in a regime of sublinear sparsity.
